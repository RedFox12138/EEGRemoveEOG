# Self-Supervised EEG Denoising via Dual-Branch Consistency Learning with Masked Reconstruction
Electroencephalogram (EEG) signals are widely used in neuroscience, clinical diagnosis, and brain-computer interface applications. However, they are easily contaminated by noise such as electromyographic (EMG) artifacts, electrooculographic (EOG) noise, and powerline interference, significantly degrading signal quality and affecting analysis accuracy. Traditional EEG denoising methods depend on prior knowledge and manual intervention, which limits their effectiveness in handling complex noise scenarios, while deep learning methods require large-scale labeled data, limiting their practicality. To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes a novel masked reconstruction self-supervised learning EEG denoising method. A dual-branch architecture is developed where the masked branch processes partially corrupted signals to learn noise-invariant representations, and the unmasked branch reconstructs global signal structures. A consistency loss is imposed to enforce coherence between the two branches’ outputs by aligning their denoised representations under varying masking conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively removes complex noise, significantly improving EEG signal quality. This work provides an efficient self-supervised learning framework for EEG denoising and a novel perspective for broader biosignal processing tasks.
